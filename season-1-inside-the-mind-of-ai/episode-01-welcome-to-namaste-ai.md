<details id="course-roadmap" open style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
<summary><strong style="font-size: 1.25em;">🗺️ The Course Roadmap</strong></summary>
<div style="margin-left: 3rem; margin-top: .25rem;">

🗺️ This course moves from the basic ideas behind AI to practical ways of building with it. Each topic gives context for the next one, so learning in sequence matters.

| | |
|:---|:---|
| **AI Fundamentals** - What AI is, how it evolved, and its real-world impact | **RAG** - Retrieval-augmented generation |
| **Large Language Models (LLMs)** - How they work at a high level | **AI Agents** - Systems that can plan and act toward a goal |
| **Prompt Engineering** - Practical techniques for working with models | **MCP** - Model Context Protocol |
| **AI for Software Developers** - Building AI-powered applications | **AI Engineering** - Designing and operating dependable AI systems |
| **AI Tools & Ecosystem** - Tools, libraries, and frameworks | **Projects** - Hands-on work throughout the series |

> **Learn in order.** A later topic is easier to understand when the earlier foundation is already familiar.

<details id="learn-slowly-and-practically" style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
<summary><strong style="font-size: 1.25em;">📖 Learn Slowly and Practically</strong></summary>
<div style="margin-left: 3rem; margin-top: .25rem;">

📖 The goal is not to finish videos quickly. The goal is to understand enough to apply an idea without needing to memorize every sentence.

| | |
|:---|:---|
| 1. Do not binge-watch | 7. Run small experiments |
| 2. Code along | 8. Break things on purpose |
| 3. Pause when an idea needs thinking time | 9. Fix what broke |
| 4. Make your own notes | 10. Keep figuring out why it worked |
| 5. Complete assignments | 11. Share and learn with the community |
| 6. Build projects | 12. Use social media thoughtfully to stay connected |

For example, after learning a prompt technique, try it with a tiny task of your own. Change one instruction, observe the response, and write down what changed. That small loop turns passive viewing into understanding.

</div>
</details>

<details id="curiosity-time-and-attention" style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
<summary><strong style="font-size: 1.25em;">🎒 Bring Curiosity, Time, and Attention</strong></summary>
<div style="margin-left: 3rem; margin-top: .25rem;">

### Curiosity

The course can give you a path, but curiosity is what makes you ask the next useful question. Build the foundation first, then use that foundation to investigate new tools and ideas as the field changes.

### Time

Do not rush to finish an episode. A one-hour video can take several hours to pause, rewatch, make notes, and try the ideas. That is study time well spent, not falling behind.

### Attention

Give the material focused time. Reduce distractions, pause to reflect, and keep a note of what you understood and what is still unclear. The title of the transformer paper, **"Attention Is All You Need,"** is a useful reminder that focused attention matters here too.

</div>
</details>

## Key Takeaways

- **The course follows a deliberate sequence** from AI foundations to projects and engineering practice. [🔗](#course-roadmap)
- **Basic programming is enough to begin**; deep ML mathematics is not a prerequisite for this course. [🔗](#prerequisites)
- **Active learning beats fast consumption**: pause, code, write notes, experiment, break, and fix. [🔗](#learn-slowly-and-practically)
- **Curiosity, time, and attention support understanding**, especially when a topic needs another pass. [🔗](#curiosity-time-and-attention)
- **Personal notes and experiments make learning reusable**, rather than leaving it as a finished video. [🔗](#mental-model)
